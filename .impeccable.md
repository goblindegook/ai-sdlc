## Design Context

### Users
A mixed internal audience: engineering leads deciding what teams should adopt, engineers using AI coding agents day
to day, and people outside engineering who want the evidence behind AI decisions. They come with a question ("should
we do X?", "what do we watch for?") and want the answer and its backing fast. They will not read the studies.

### Brand Personality
Practical, direct, evidence-backed. A field guide, not a report: tells you what to do, what to watch, and why, in
plain words. Not an official company site: no employer or vendor branding.

### Site Structure
- **Overview**: the agent loop diagram, then Adopt and Avoid by topic. Nothing under the diagram restates it. Trial items are not listed here, only linked per topic: too much unproven material for the main page.
- **Recommendations**: Adopt and Avoid, each item with a summary, "Watch for" signals and its evidence.
- **Trials**: ideas worth testing that the evidence doesn't back yet, same item layout.
- **Challenges**: problems the recommendations don't solve, and questions the evidence can't settle yet, in one list.
- **Evidence**: every cited source, strongest kind first. The only page that links out to other sites.

### Aesthetic Direction
Tech-radar framing: Adopt / Trial / Avoid, quick to scan, without copying any published radar's look. Light and dark
themes. Archivo for headings, Atkinson Hyperlegible Next for text, on warm-tinted neutrals. Ruled lists, not cards.
Anti-references: decorative gradients, purple, rainbow colour, pulsing badges, rounded "fingernail" cards, emoji,
Inter / JetBrains Mono defaults, `//` motifs, glassmorphism, hype taglines, a grey subtitle under every heading.
Link previews carry no image.

### Design Principles
1. Answer first: the recommendation and what to watch come before the evidence; study detail lives on the Evidence
   page. Summaries are practical, with no study names or figures.
2. Scannable: short titles and summaries, items folded to their title until opened, Expand all for reading at length.
   No counts of items or sources anywhere, no intros or manifestos, ring rules one short sentence, the date once in
   the footer.
3. Consistency across pages: one item pattern (folded title with topics, body with summary, signals and evidence)
   and one way to do each thing everywhere.
4. One filter model on every list: single-choice topic tabs with All, a search, and Expand all, kept in the URL
   (`?topic=`, `?q=`). The filter bar stays pinned while scrolling; on phones the header scrolls away instead.
5. Colour carries meaning only (the three rings), and never alone: every ring also has a text label.
6. WCAG 2.2 AA: contrast, keyboard use, visible focus, reduced motion respected. No controls nested in controls:
   topics are plain text in a folded title and become filter links once the item opens. Lists made only of links
   underline on hover and focus; links in running text are always underlined.

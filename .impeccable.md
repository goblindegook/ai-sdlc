## Design Context

### Users
A mixed internal audience: engineering leads deciding what teams should adopt, engineers using AI coding agents day
to day, and people outside engineering who want the evidence behind AI decisions. They come with a question ("should
we do X?", "what do we watch for?") and want the answer and its backing fast. They will not read the studies.

### Brand Personality
Practical, direct, evidence-backed. A field guide, not a report: tells you what to do, what to watch, and why, in
plain words. Not an official company site: no employer or vendor branding.

### Aesthetic Direction
Tech-radar framing: Adopt / Trial / Avoid, quick to scan, without copying any published radar's look. Light and dark
themes. Anti-references: the AI-slop tells catalogued in the wiki (concepts/ai-slop-ui-tells): decorative gradients,
purple, rainbow colour, pulsing badges, rounded "fingernail" cards, emoji, Inter / JetBrains Mono defaults, `//`
motifs, glassmorphism, hype taglines, a grey subtitle under every heading.

### Design Principles
1. Answer first: the recommendation and what to watch come before the evidence; study detail lives on the Evidence page.
2. Scannable over readable-at-length: short summaries, clear ring grouping, counts that orient.
3. Colour carries meaning only (the three rings), and never alone: every ring also has a text label.
4. One filter model everywhere: the eight area toggles behave the same on every list.
5. WCAG 2.2 AA: contrast, keyboard use, visible focus, reduced motion respected.
